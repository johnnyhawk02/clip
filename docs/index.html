PhotoSound

A small Mac sound editor for one job: chopping up loops, keeping the good bits and editing them into a track. Tracks work like layers in a photo editor: only one clip is heard at a time, and the top layer wins. (It was called Sound Edit until October 2026.)

macOSWAV · MP3 · FLAC · OGG · M4A · AIFF · CAF inWAV · AIFF outExports to Ableton Live 12

01Getting started

  1. Open the app from Applications, or Spotlight "PhotoSound".
  2. Drop loops on the window. Each goes on its own layer. A tempo in the file name, like "118bpm 16bar" or "124 BPM", sets the song tempo and stretches the loop to fit it.
  3. Press Space to play the whole song from the playhead. Click empty space to move the playhead.
  4. Chop, stack and arrange with the tools below, then File ▸ Save Project ⌘S to keep everything in one file.
Nothing you do touches your original files. Every edit can be undone with ⌘Z.

02The arrangement

A song is a stack of layers, and audio sits on them as clips, laid out much like Ableton's Arrangement view. The top bar sets the tempo, beats per bar, the grid, and whether things snap to it.

  • Select time as in Live: drag across empty space, or across a clip's waveform, and a light blue wash marks that stretch on the layers you drag over. ⌫ deletes it, ⌘E splits the clips at its ends, and ⌘L loops it. Mangle, BBCut, Reverse, Swap and the other clip processes work only inside it too. ⌘D duplicates it right after itself; Edit ▸ Duplicate Time ⇧⌘D pushes the rest of the song later, Delete Time ⇧⌘⌫ takes it out and closes the gap, and Insert Time ⌘I opens up empty time. Shift-click clips to select whole clips.
  • Move a clip by dragging its name strip, along its layer or onto another. Drag below the last layer to make a new one. ⌥-drag drags a copy.
  • Trim by dragging either edge. The audio underneath is kept, so you can drag it back out.
  • Phrase lines: a fine line runs across every 4 bars, counting from bar 1. PhotoSound ▸ Preferences ⌘, changes the spacing, or 0 turns them off.
  • Snapping: moves and trims snap to the grid, other clips' edges and the playhead. Hold ⌘ to place freely.
  • Fades: drag the small handles at a clip's top corners to fade it in or out.
  • Locators: Control ▸ Add Locator, or right-click the ruler, adds a named marker. [ ] jump between them; right-click one to rename it, delete it or loop to the next.
  • Playing: play starts from where you last clicked and stopping leaves it there, as in Live. ⇧Space carries on from where you stopped, ⌘F turns Follow on or off, the Metronome button in the top bar clicks along (higher on beat 1, never exported), and ⌘U quantizes clip starts to the grid.
  • More Live editing: ⇧⌥-drag a clip's waveform to slide its audio inside it. Edit ▸ Cut / Copy / Paste Time (⇧⌘X ⇧⌘C ⇧⌘V) work on every layer. ⇧⌘J crops clips to a selection and ⌘J consolidates it. 0 switches clips off and on. Arrow keys nudge clips or move the insert marker; ⇧← ⇧→ grow the selection; ⌘↑ ⌘↓ double or halve the loop. Double-click the bar numbers to zoom; H fits the layers to the height. Drag locators to move them, ⌘R renames the selected one. The Clip View has Pitch and ×2 / ÷2.
  • Detail view: select one clip and it opens under the song, as in Live: its waveform with the bar ruler and start flag, and the Clip View down the left (Warp and the clip's BPM, Loop, Reverse, Start, Length, Gain). Click in the waveform to edit there; click the song to go back. The transport sits along the top.
  • Each layer has an eye (show/hide), a colour swatch and a volume slider. ⌥⌘↑ ⌥⌘↓ step between layers.
  • Copy, cut, paste clips with ⌘C ⌘X ⌘V. Paste goes on the selected layer at the playhead. ⌘D duplicates a clip right after itself, and ⌫ deletes it.
  • Dropping files onto a layer adds them at the drop point. Several files go on the layers below, one each.

03Layers

Only one clip is heard at any moment: the one on the highest layer that has a clip there. Where no layer has a clip you hear silence. So you edit one track instead of mixing several. Lay a long loop on a low layer, then drop short clips on the layers above to patch over parts of it.

  • Washed-out parts of a clip are covered by a layer above, so the screen always shows what you hear.
  • The eye hides a layer, and the layers below show through.
  • Move Layer Up / Down on a layer's right-click menu, or the Layers menu, changes which one wins.
  • Merge Down joins a layer with the one below into a single layer that sounds the same. Each clip is cut down to the part you heard, so it all stays editable.
  • No clicks: where a layer cuts in over one that is still sounding, the one cut off fades out over 5 ms. A clip that starts or ends on its own keeps its edge.
  • Recording puts the take on a new top layer. Layers ▸ Flatten Layers turns what you hear into one plain clip.

04Chopping loops

Split Clip ⌘ECuts the clip under the pointer, at the pointer (snapped to the grid), or at the playhead when the pointer isn't over a clip.
Split tool CClick anywhere on a clip to cut it there. Press S to go back to the selection tool.
Slice at Grid ⌥⌘UCuts the selected clips at every grid line, ready to drag around, duplicate, loop and group.
Reverse RPlays the selected clips backwards. Also on the clip's right-click menu.

05Mangle and BBCut

Mangle rearranges the selected clips in the slots they already sit in. A single clip is sliced at the grid first. The buttons are on the floating tool palette, and in Clip ▸ Mangle.

MangleWhat happens
ShuffleThe slices swap places at random.
ReverseThe slices play in reverse order.
SwapNeighbouring pairs swap.
StutterSome slices repeat the one before.
FlipSome slices play backwards.
DropSome slices go silent.
Again ⌘YRe-rolls the last mangle for a fresh variation.

BBCut

Breakbeat cutting: the selection is re-cut into blocks, some jump to other parts of the loop, and some become stutter rolls. Each style has its own feel.

Mangling a stretch of time

Drag along the thin strip at the top of the ruler to set a loop range, which turns Loop on. Loops always cover whole bars. Drag the black brace to move the whole loop, or grab either end to resize it. Drag in the bar numbers below the strip to zoom: down zooms in, up zooms out, sideways scrolls. Mangle then cuts the clips at the loop's edges, slices what's inside at the grid and mangles only that, on the layer you're working on or the layers of the selected clips. You hear it looping, and ⌘Y gives another take.

06Building a track

The Loop Pool

Open it with ⌘7 or View ▸ Panels ▸ Loop Pool. It fills itself with the loops in your song, grouped clips included, all fitted to the song tempo.

  • Drag a loop onto any layer. It lands on the grid line under the pointer. Drop it below the last layer for a new layer at the bottom. Drop it on a layer above a loop to patch over it.
  • Add to Pool on a clip's right-click menu puts that exact clip in, trimmed or grouped.
  • × takes a loop out. Add Loops from Song brings in any new ones.

Swap a section ⇧R

Click a clip and press ⇧R. It becomes a random section of the same length from the pool, in the same place. Press ⇧R again for another, or ⌘Z to go back. Works on several selected clips at once.

Auto Arrange

Press Auto Arrange in the top bar. It builds about four minutes from the pool: each loop is cut into 8 or 16 bar sections, and one section plays at a time, end to end on a single layer. Calm loops fill the quiet parts and busy ones the peaks, following an intro, a build, a breakdown a little past halfway, a second peak and an outro.

  • ◀ ▶ flip through variations. Each number always gives the same layout, so you can go back to one you liked.
  • 8 / 16 bars changes the section length.
  • Loops shorter than a section repeat to fill it.
  • It replaces the whole song, and ⌘Z brings it back.

Dropping clips on clips

Clips on a layer never overlap, as in Live. Drop, paste or drag a clip onto another clip on the same layer and it cuts a gap in the one underneath, the length of the clip you dropped. To hear one clip over another, put it on a layer above.

Drag a clip's edge over its neighbour instead and the two overlap and crossfade across the overlap, keeping the level even. An X marks each crossfade.

Every other clip edge gets a tiny fade, as in Live: none where the audio crosses zero at the edge, so a kick or snare keeps its snap, and up to 4 ms where the edge cuts through a loud part of the wave and would click.

07Tempo and warping

  • Tempo from names: a clip whose name gives its tempo ("Loop 118bpm 16bar", "Kick - 124 BPM", "Bpm130_Drums") follows the song tempo. A bar count in the name gives the exact tempo.
  • Change the tempo by dragging the BPM box up or down (1 BPM a step, 0.20 with ⇧), or click it to type. Every clip stays on its bar and the view keeps the same bars; only the time each bar takes changes.
  • Re-Pitch: warped clips speed up and pitch up with the tempo, like a record, and you hear it live while you drag. PhotoSound ▸ Preferences ⌘, ▸ Keep pitch stretches them instead.
  • "no warp" in a clip's name strip means it plays at its own speed and doesn't follow the tempo. Right-click ▸ Warp to Tempo fixes that.
  • Set Tempo from Clip reads a loop's length as a whole number of bars and sets the song to match.
  • Warp to Tempo switches warping on or off for the selected clips. Clip ▸ Warp Loops on Import turns the automatic part off.
  • Grid: Auto, 1 Bar, 1/2 down to 1/32, or Off. With the grid on, the ruler shows bars and beats. ⌘4 toggles snapping.

08Loops and groups

  • Loop Clip ⌥⌘L makes a clip repeat for as long as you drag its right edge out. Dotted lines show where it starts over.
  • Song loop: drag in the ruler to draw a loop range, and turn it on or off with ⌘L or the loop button.
  • Group into Clip ⌘G: shift-click clips (or ⌘A) and they become one clip holding them all. Double-click it to open it as its own arrangement; the path bar or ⌘Return goes back up. ⇧⌘G ungroups.
  • Collapse to Audio ⌘J renders a group, looped or trimmed clip down to one plain clip that sounds the same.

09The clip editor

The ruler works like the song view's: bars and beats from the clip's start, a loop strip along the top (whole bars only), and drag the bar numbers to zoom. The black flag is the start marker, as in Live. Drag it and the clip plays from there, looping round so the audio before it comes at the end, while the waveform stays where it is. Right-click the flag and choose Set 1.1.1 Here to count bars from it, with -1, -2 before it.

Double-click a clip, or press Return, to edit its audio in the waveform editor. ◀ Arrangement or ⌘Return goes back, and ⇧Tab flips between the two as in Live.

ToolKeyWhat it does

The older sound-editor features live under the More menu: effects (Normalize, fades, Reverb, Delay, Echo, EQ, Compressor, Noise Gate, Pitch, Tempo and more), Insert (silence, tones, noise, FM synthesis), cue points and regions, recording, Spectrum and Batch Convert.

10Headphone crossfeed

The Master strip under the layers has the output volume (double-click to reset), its level meters and a headphone button. The headphone button, or Control ▸ Headphone Crossfeed, makes headphones sound like a pair of speakers in front of you. Each ear also hears a slightly delayed, duller share of the other side, the way it would in a room, so hard-panned sounds stop sitting inside your head and the bass comes out in the middle.

The filters were measured from a CanOpener setting by running clicks through each side, and match it to within -100 dB. It only changes what you hear, never what you export, and it stays on between runs.

11Saving and exporting

Save Project ⌘SOne .sndproj file with every layer, clip, group, loop, setting and the audio itself. Double-click it in Finder to open.
Export ⇧⌘RWhat the whole song sounds like as one WAV or AIFF, at 16-bit, 24-bit or 32-bit float.
Export Ableton Live SetA Live 12 project folder. The layers are flattened onto one track, each clip cut to the part you hear, with loops and warping kept. Crossfades don't carry over.
